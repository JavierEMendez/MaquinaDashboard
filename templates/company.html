{# ── Toll economics: model tarifa vs título-implied, per passage ── #}
Toll Economics — Model Tarifa vs Título-Implied, per Vehicle Passage
What each highway charges per vehicle versus what the concession title assumed. Live — responds to the sliders above.
YearHighway Passages (M)Model tarifaTítulo-implied GapHurdle reachedPromote / passage
What this table is doing

The model tarifa is Valoran's projected revenue for the year divided by the vehicles projected to pass — the average toll actually collected per vehicle, across all classes.

The título-implied figure is the concession title's contractual revenue for that year divided by the same vehicle count. The título is only a peso total — it contains no traffic and no tariff — so this converts it into per-vehicle terms to make the two comparable. It is a derived comparison, not a rate anyone charges.

The gap is the excess as a % of the baseline — and that percentage is precisely what the hurdle ladder measures, so the "hurdle reached" column shows where each road's year lands.

Why it matters

It reduces the whole deal to one number per road per year: how much more revenue per vehicle the model assumes than the concession promised. That gap is the entire source of Maquina's promote — no gap, no payment.

It also shows why v2 is worth so much less than v1: a ~30–35% gap on Ventura barely clears Hurdle 1, while Pitahaya's ~11% gap sits deep inside it at 5%. Under v1's forecast the gap was 120–350%, landing most of the excess in Hurdle 4 at 75%.

The diligence question: is a ~30% revenue-per-vehicle premium over the título realistic? That single judgement drives the valuation more than any other input — test it with the toll-level sliders.
